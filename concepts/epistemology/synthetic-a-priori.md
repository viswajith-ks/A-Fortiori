# Synthetic A Priori

**Category:** epistemology

## Definition
Synthetic a priori knowledge consists of propositions that are both informative about the world (synthetic) and knowable independently of particular experience (a priori).

## Description
[Kant's](../../thinkers/kant.md) concept of synthetic a priori knowledge revolutionarily challenged the traditional distinction between analytic truths (known by analysis of concepts) and synthetic truths (known by experience). Kant argued that some knowledge is both substantive about reality and knowable through [reason](../meta-philosophy/reason.md) alone, providing the foundation for mathematics, pure natural science, and metaphysics.

## The Traditional Distinction

### Analytic vs Synthetic (Content)
- **Analytic propositions**: Truth follows from analysis of concepts
  - "All bachelors are unmarried men"
  - Predicate contained in subject concept
  - Cannot be denied without contradiction
  
- **Synthetic propositions**: Truth depends on how world actually is
  - "All bachelors are unhappy"
  - Predicate adds new information about subject
  - Can be denied without logical contradiction

### A Priori vs A Posteriori (Justification)
- **A priori knowledge**: Known independently of particular experience
  - Logic, mathematics, conceptual truths
  - Universal and necessary
  - Available to reason alone
  
- **A posteriori knowledge**: Known through empirical experience
  - Observational sciences, particular facts
  - Contingent and revisable
  - Depends on sensory input

## Kant's Innovation

### Four-Fold Classification
1. **Analytic a priori**: Traditional logical and mathematical truths
2. **Analytic a posteriori**: Impossible (contradictory)
3. **Synthetic a posteriori**: Empirical knowledge from experience
4. **Synthetic a priori**: Kant's revolutionary category

### Examples of Synthetic A Priori

#### Mathematical Knowledge
- **Arithmetic**: 6 + 9 = 15
  - Synthetic: "15" not contained in concept "6 + 9"
  - A priori: Known without counting particular objects
  - Construction in intuition: Mathematical proof involves constructing objects in pure intuition

- **Geometry**: Straight line between two points is shortest
  - Synthetic: "Shortest" adds spatial information to "straight line"
  - A priori: Known without measuring particular distances
  - Pure intuition of space: Spatial relationships given in form of intuition

#### Pure Natural Science
- **Substance principle**: In all changes, substance is conserved
- **Causal principle**: Every event has a cause
- **Reciprocity principle**: All substances are in mutual interaction
- **Mathematical principles**: Conservation laws, geometrical properties of space

#### Metaphysical Principles
- **Unity of consciousness**: All my representations belong to unified consciousness
- **Temporal necessity**: All events occur in time
- **Spatial necessity**: All outer objects exist in space
- **Categorical principles**: Principles governing concepts like substance, causation

## Kant's Arguments

### Transcendental Deduction
1. **Self-consciousness requires unity** of experience
2. **Unity requires synthesis** according to rules (categories)
3. **Categories apply necessarily** to all objects of experience
4. **Therefore**: Categorical principles are synthetic a priori

### Transcendental Aesthetic
1. **Space and time** are pure forms of intuition
2. **Not empirical concepts** derived from experience
3. **Conditions for possibility** of outer and inner experience
4. **Therefore**: Spatial and temporal principles are synthetic a priori

### Mathematical Construction
1. **Mathematical concepts** constructed in pure intuition
2. **Not analytic** because construction adds content
3. **Not empirical** because construction is a priori
4. **Therefore**: Mathematics is synthetic a priori

## Historical Context

### Rationalist Background
- **Descartes**: Mathematics provides model for all knowledge
- **Spinoza**: Geometric method in ethics
- **Leibniz**: Analytic view of mathematical truth
- **Problem**: How can reason alone give substantive knowledge?

### Empiricist Challenge
- **Locke**: All knowledge comes from experience
- **Hume**: Mathematical relations of ideas vs matters of fact
- **Hume's Fork**: Either analytic (uninformative) or synthetic (uncertain)
- **Problem**: Mathematics seems both certain and informative

### Kant's Synthesis
- **Third way**: Between rationalist dogmatism and empiricist skepticism
- **Limits of reason**: Synthetic a priori limited to possible experience
- **Transcendental idealism**: We know phenomena, not noumena
- **Critical philosophy**: Establishing scope and limits of synthetic a priori knowledge

## Contemporary Debates

### Logical Positivist Critique
- **Vienna Circle**: All meaningful statements either analytic or empirical
- **Mathematics as analytic**: Mathematical truths are elaborate tautologies
- **Protocol sentences**: Synthetic statements must be empirically verifiable
- **Rejection**: No synthetic a priori knowledge exists

### Quine's Challenge
- **"Two Dogmas of Empiricism"**: Analytic/synthetic distinction is untenable
- **Holistic confirmation**: Theories face tribunal of experience as wholes
- **Pragmatic criteria**: Retain beliefs that prove most useful
- **Web of belief**: No sharp distinction between conceptual and empirical

### Post-Quinean Developments
- **Putnam**: Some statements (like "Cats are animals") seem analytic
- **Kripke**: Necessary a posteriori and contingent a priori statements
- **Two-dimensional semantics**: Primary vs secondary intensions
- **Neo-Kantian**: Revival of synthetic a priori in various forms

## Types of Candidates

### Logical and Mathematical
- **Basic logical principles**: Law of non-contradiction, excluded middle
- **Mathematical axioms**: Peano axioms, geometric postulates
- **Set-theoretic principles**: Axiom of choice, continuum hypothesis
- **Problem**: Developments in logic and mathematics challenge necessity

### Conceptual Truths
- **Sortals**: "Nothing is red all over and green all over"
- **Personal identity**: Psychological or bodily continuity conditions
- **Mental concepts**: "Pain is unpleasant," "Belief aims at truth"
- **Problem**: Cross-cultural variation suggests empirical origin

### Scientific Principles
- **Conservation laws**: Energy, momentum conservation
- **Symmetry principles**: Spatial and temporal homogeneity
- **Quantum principles**: Uncertainty principle, quantum logic
- **Problem**: Scientific revolutions challenge supposed necessity

### Moral and Aesthetic
- **Categorical imperative**: Act only on universalizable maxims
- **Human dignity**: Persons have inherent worth
- **Aesthetic judgment**: Beauty involves purposiveness without purpose
- **Problem**: Cultural relativism and moral disagreement

## Arguments For Synthetic A Priori

### Transcendental Arguments
- **Conditions of possibility**: Some principles necessary for experience
- **Self-refutation**: Denying principles undermines conditions for denial
- **Universal features**: All rational beings must accept certain principles
- **Examples**: Causation necessary for temporal experience

### Intuitive Knowledge
- **Rational intuition**: Direct insight into necessary truths
- **Mathematical insight**: Seeing that theorems must be true
- **Moral insight**: Recognition of fundamental moral principles
- **Aesthetic insight**: Immediate recognition of beauty

### Conceptual Analysis
- **Deep analysis**: Some conceptual connections not immediately obvious
- **Philosophical analysis**: Reveals necessary connections between concepts
- **Thought experiments**: Test conceptual intuitions about necessity
- **Examples**: Personal identity, free will, consciousness

## Arguments Against Synthetic A Priori

### Naturalistic Challenge
- **Evolutionary epistemology**: All knowledge has evolutionary origins
- **Neurological basis**: Brain mechanisms explain apparent necessity
- **Cultural variation**: Different cultures have different "necessary" principles
- **Scientific revision**: Even basic principles subject to empirical revision

### Semantic Challenge
- **Meaning holism**: No sharp boundaries between concepts
- **Theory-ladenness**: All observation influenced by theoretical commitments
- **Incommensurability**: Paradigm shifts change meanings of terms
- **Translation**: Difficulty translating between theoretical frameworks

### Pragmatic Challenge
- **Instrumental value**: Principles retained because useful, not true
- **Fallibilism**: All knowledge revisable in light of experience
- **Coherentist epistemology**: No foundational synthetic a priori principles
- **Democratic epistemology**: Community determines what counts as knowledge
