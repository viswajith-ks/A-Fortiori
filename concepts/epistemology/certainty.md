# Certainty

**Category:** epistemology

## Definition

Certainty is the state of being completely confident that a belief or knowledge claim is true, with no possibility of error or doubt.

## Description

Certainty has been both a goal and a problem in philosophy. While we naturally seek certainty in our [knowledge](knowledge.md), philosophical analysis reveals how difficult certainty is to achieve. This tension drives much of epistemology and connects to [skepticism](skepticism.md) and the search for [foundations](../meta-philosophy/foundation.md).

## Types of Certainty

### Psychological Certainty

Subjective feeling of complete confidence:

- "I am certain it will rain today"
- Based on personal conviction, not logical necessity
- Can be mistaken—strong feeling doesn't guarantee truth

### Epistemic Certainty  

Objective warrant for complete confidence:

- Evidence or reasoning that rules out all doubt
- Truth cannot be otherwise given the evidence
- Much harder to achieve than psychological certainty

### Logical Certainty

Truth that follows necessarily from logical principles:

- Mathematical proofs, tautologies
- "2 + 2 = 4" or "All bachelors are unmarried"
- Based on meaning and logical relationships

### Practical Certainty

Confidence sufficient for action despite theoretical doubt:

- "I'm certain this bridge will hold me"
- Reasonable confidence for practical purposes
- Acknowledges theoretical possibility of error

## Historical Approaches

### Cartesian Certainty

Descartes sought indubitable foundations through [methodological doubt](../meta-philosophy/methodological-doubt.md):

- Doubt everything possible
- Find what cannot be doubted (*cogito ergo sum*)
- Build knowledge on certain foundations

### Empiricist Certainty

British empiricists located certainty in immediate experience:

- Direct awareness of sense-data
- Introspective awareness of mental states
- Relations between ideas (Hume)

### Kantian Certainty

Kant argued for certainty about conditions of possible experience:

- Synthetic a priori knowledge
- Transcendental arguments for necessary conditions
- Certainty about structure, not specific content

## Challenges to Certainty

### [Skeptical Arguments](skepticism.md)

- **Cartesian demon**: Systematic deception possible
- **Brain in vat**: Experience could be entirely simulated
- **Dreaming**: How distinguish waking from dreaming?

### [Gettier Problems](knowledge.md)

- Justified true belief can fail to be knowledge
- Even seemingly certain beliefs may be accidentally true
- Challenges connection between certainty and knowledge

### Fallibilism

Position that certainty is impossible or unnecessary:

- All beliefs could be mistaken in principle
- Knowledge doesn't require certainty
- Reasonable confidence sufficient for knowledge

## Certainty and Doubt

### Methodological Doubt

Temporary suspension of belief to find certainty:

- Cartesian strategy for finding foundations
- Systematic rather than genuine doubt
- Tool for philosophical investigation

### Reasonable Doubt

Standard that allows action without certainty:

- Legal standard ("beyond reasonable doubt")
- Practical epistemology
- Balances skepticism with need to act

### Pyrrhonian Suspension

Complete suspension of judgment on disputed questions:

- Ancient skeptical strategy
- Seeks mental tranquility (*ataraxia*)
- Avoids dogmatism about certainty

## Degrees of Certainty

Rather than all-or-nothing, certainty may come in degrees:

- **High certainty**: Mathematical truths, logic
- **Moderate certainty**: Well-established scientific facts  
- **Low certainty**: Historical claims, predictions
- **Uncertainty**: Disputed philosophical questions

## Relationship to Other Concepts

### [Truth](truth.md)

- Certainty aims at truth but doesn't guarantee it
- Truth is objective; certainty is epistemic
- Can be certain of false things, uncertain of true things

### [Justification](justification.md)  

- Strong justification may approach certainty
- But justification admits degrees; certainty seems absolute
- Gettier cases show justified belief can fail

### [Belief](belief.md)

- Certainty is maximum degree of belief
- But belief can exist without certainty
- Psychological vs epistemic aspects

## Contemporary Views

### Contextualism

Standards for certainty vary by context:

- Everyday contexts: Lower standards
- Philosophical contexts: Higher standards  
- Scientific contexts: Empirical standards

### Relevant Alternatives

Certainty requires ruling out relevant alternatives:

- Not all logical possibilities need elimination
- Context determines which alternatives matter
- Pragmatic approach to skeptical scenarios

## Significance for A Fortiori

The agnostic approach exemplifies appropriate caution about certainty:

- Evaluate arguments without requiring certainty
- Distinguish between strong evidence and proof
- Recognize limits of human knowledge while still reasoning carefully
