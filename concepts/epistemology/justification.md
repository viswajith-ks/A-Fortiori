# Justification

**Category:** epistemology

## Definition
Justification is the process or quality by which a belief is supported by adequate reason or evidence, making it rational to hold.

## Description
In the traditional analysis of knowledge ([JTB](knowledge.md)), justification is what separates mere true belief from knowledge. It can be based on evidence, logic, experience, or authority.  
- Internalist theories see justification as accessible to the thinker’s mind.
- Externalist theories allow factors outside the thinker’s awareness (e.g., reliability).

## Main Approaches
- **Foundationalism:** All justified beliefs rest on indubitable foundations.
- **Coherentism:** Beliefs are justified by fitting well into a web of other beliefs.
- **Reliabilism:** Beliefs justified if formed by reliable processes.

## Problems
- [Regress problem](../../arguments/transcendental-arguments.md): Every reason needs another reason.  
- Gettier cases question whether justification can guarantee knowledge.
