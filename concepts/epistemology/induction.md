# Induction

**Category:** epistemology

## Definition

Induction is a form of reasoning where one infers general principles or future events from specific observations or past experience.

## Description

Unlike deduction, which guarantees the truth of its conclusion if premises are true, **induction only suggests that the conclusion is probable** based on observed patterns. Most scientific and everyday reasoning is inductive—inferring the behavior of nature from repeated experience.

## Types of Induction

- **Enumerative induction:** Generalizes from observed cases (“All observed swans are white”).
- **Statistical induction:** Uses quantitative data to predict probabilities.
- **Abductive inference:** Infers the best explanation for a set of facts.

## Justification and Limits

See [The Problem of Induction](../../arguments/problem-of-induction.md) for skepticism about induction’s rational force.

- Induction relies on assumptions about uniformity of nature.
- It is always vulnerable to “black swan” surprises.
