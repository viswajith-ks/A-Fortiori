# Perception

**Category:** epistemology

## Definition
Perception is the process by which living beings acquire knowledge through the senses—sight, hearing, touch, taste, and smell.

## Philosophical Issues
- **Source of Knowledge:** Most empirical knowledge begins with perception.
- **Reliability:** Perception can be subject to illusions, hallucinations, and error.
- **Indirect Realism (Representationalism):** We are aware of sense-data, not objects themselves.
- **Direct Realism (Naive Realism):** Perception puts us into direct contact with external reality.
- **Idealism:** Reality is fundamentally mental; perception creates objects.

## Major Questions
- Is perception a reliable basis for [knowledge](knowledge.md)?
- How can perception be justified ([justification](justification.md))?
- Are there “givens”—perceptions that require no interpretation ([foundation](../meta-philosophy/foundation.md))?
- To what degree does the mind construct or filter perceptual experience?
