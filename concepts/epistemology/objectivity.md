# Objectivity

**Category:** epistemology

## Definition

Objectivity is the quality of being independent from personal biases, emotions, and subjective perspectives—grounded in facts that hold irrespective of individuals' beliefs.

## Philosophical Significance

- **In Epistemology:** Objectivity is a criterion for knowledge ([knowledge](knowledge.md)), truth ([truth](truth.md)), and ([justification](justification.md)).
- **In Science:** Standard for reliable observation, experiment, and theory.
- **In Ethics:** Related to whether moral truths exist “out there” or are constructed.

## Major Issues

- **Relativism:** Are all perspectives equally valid? If so, objectivity is undermined.
- **Intersubjectivity:** Objective knowledge may be defined as what rational observers can agree upon.
- **Theory-Ladenness:** All observations may be influenced by prior beliefs or conceptual schemes.

## Approaches

- **Realism:** Claims objective facts/truths exist.
- **Anti-Realism:** Denies mind-independent reality.
- **Constructivism:** Objectivity is the product of social rational processes.

## In Practice

- **Objectivity in Science:** Peer review, reproducibility, statistical analysis.
- **Philosophy:** Argumentation, logical rigor, dialectic.
- **Law:** Impartial judgement, standards of evidence.
