# Logic

**Category:** logic

## Definition
Logic is the systematic study of the principles of valid inference and correct reasoning, examining the structure of arguments and the conditions under which conclusions follow from premises.

## Description
Logic investigates the formal patterns that distinguish good reasoning from bad reasoning. It abstracts away from the specific content of arguments to focus on their structural properties—whether conclusions genuinely follow from their premises. As Aristotle noted, logic is a tool (*organon*) for acquiring knowledge rather than a body of knowledge itself.

Logic serves as both a descriptive discipline (studying how people actually reason) and a normative one (establishing standards for how people should reason). Modern logic has developed formal symbolic languages to represent arguments with mathematical precision.

## Branches of Logic

### Formal vs Informal Logic
- **Formal Logic**: Uses symbolic languages and mathematical methods (propositional logic, predicate logic)
- **Informal Logic**: Analyzes arguments in natural language, focusing on fallacies and practical reasoning

### Deductive vs Inductive Logic  
- **Deductive Logic**: Studies arguments where conclusions necessarily follow from premises
- **Inductive Logic**: Studies arguments where premises provide probabilistic support for conclusions

### Classical vs Non-Classical Logic
- **Classical Logic**: Based on traditional principles ([Classical Logical Axioms](classical-logical-axioms.md))
- **Non-Classical Logic**: Modifies or rejects some classical principles (modal, many-valued, relevance logics)

## Core Concepts
Logic examines several fundamental relationships:

- **[Validity](validity.md)**: Whether an argument's structure guarantees the truth of its conclusion given true premises
- **[Soundness](soundness.md)**: Whether an argument is both valid and has true premises  
- **[Deduction](deduction.md)**: Reasoning from general principles to specific conclusions
- **[Inference](inference.md)**: The process of drawing conclusions from premises
- **[Proof](proof.md)**: A sequence of valid inferences establishing a conclusion

## Historical Development
- **Ancient**: Aristotle's *Organon* established formal logic as a discipline
- **Medieval**: Scholastics developed modal logic and theories of supposition
- **Modern**: Frege, Russell, and others created mathematical logic and formal systems
- **Contemporary**: Computer science applications, non-classical logics, cognitive studies

## Applications
- **Philosophy**: Evaluating philosophical arguments and theories
- **Mathematics**: Providing foundations for mathematical proof
- **Computer Science**: Programming languages, artificial intelligence, databases  
- **Law**: Legal reasoning and argumentation
- **Science**: Hypothesis formation and testing methodologies

## Philosophical Significance
Logic raises deep questions about the nature of reasoning itself:
- Are logical principles discovered features of reality or useful human constructions?
- What makes one form of reasoning better than another?
- How do logical systems relate to natural language and thought?

## Key Questions
- Is logic descriptive (how we think) or normative (how we should think)?
- Are there universal logical principles or are they culturally relative?
- What is the relationship between logic and truth?
