# Buddhist Logic

**Category:** logic

## Definition

Buddhist logic refers to the systems of reasoning developed in Buddhist philosophy, especially in Indian traditions like Dignaga and Dharmakirti, which differ significantly from Western classical logic.

## Key Features

- **Tetralemma (Catuskoti):** Fourfold alternatives: something can be true, not true, both, or neither.
- **Rejects Principle of Explosion:** Some contradictions are not fatal for reasoning.
- **Pramana Theory:** Knowledge judged by means of valid cognition (perception and inference).

## Contrast with Western Logic

- Western logic typically operates on bivalent truth (true/false) and excludes contradictions.
- Buddhist logic recognizes complex truth conditions, context dependence, and “middle ways.”
- Emphasis on practical soteriology: logic as a means to end suffering.

## Major Applications

- Debates in Buddhist epistemology, especially concerning perception and inference.
- Dialogues on emptiness, self, and causality.
- Challenging dogmatism and rigid categories.

## Philosophical Relevance

Shows the cultural plurality of logic, the possibility of meaningful reasoning with contradictions, and the philosophical function of logic beyond mere truth-tracking.
