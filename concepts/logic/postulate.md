# Postulate

**Category:** logic

## Definition

A postulate is a statement that is assumed to be true without proof as a starting point for mathematical or logical reasoning, typically within a specific system or domain.

## Description

While closely related to [axioms](axiom.md), postulates are often domain-specific assumptions that may be less universal or self-evident than logical axioms. The term is most commonly used in mathematics and geometry, where postulates establish the basic properties of the objects being studied.

## Postulate vs Axiom

### Traditional Distinction

- **Axioms**: Universal principles of reasoning ([Classical Logical Axioms](classical-logical-axioms.md))
- **Postulates**: Specific assumptions about particular subject matter
- **Axioms**: Self-evident truths of reason
- **Postulates**: Working assumptions that may be stipulative

### Modern Usage

- **Often used interchangeably** in contemporary mathematics and logic
- **Context-dependent**: Same statement may be axiom in one system, postulate in another
- **Systematic approach**: Both serve as foundational premises for [deduction](deduction.md)

## Famous Examples

### Euclidean Geometry

**Euclid's Five Postulates:**

1. A straight line can be drawn from any point to any point
2. A finite straight line can be extended continuously in a straight line
3. A circle can be described with any center and distance
4. All right angles are equal to one another
5. Parallel Postulate: If two lines are cut by a transversal so that interior angles sum to less than 180°, the lines will meet on that side

### Non-Euclidean Geometry

- **Rejection of Fifth Postulate**: Led to hyperbolic and elliptic geometries
- **Alternative postulates**: Different assumptions about parallel lines
- **Consistency**: Multiple geometric systems equally valid mathematically
- **Empirical question**: Which geometry describes physical space?

### Set Theory

**Zermelo-Fraenkel Axioms** (often called postulates):

- Extensionality, regularity, pairing, union, infinity, replacement, power set
- **Choice Axiom**: Controversial additional postulate
- **Foundation for mathematics**: Most mathematics derivable from these postulates

## Role in Mathematical Systems

### Foundational Function

- **Starting points** for mathematical [proof](proof.md)
- **Define the domain**: Establish what objects exist and their basic properties
- **Enable derivation**: Allow construction of theorems through [valid inference](inference.md)
- **Systematic organization**: Create coherent mathematical theories

### Independence and Consistency

- **Independence**: Postulates should not be derivable from others
- **Consistency**: Postulates should not lead to [contradictions](contradiction.md)
- **Completeness**: Ideally, postulates determine truth of all statements in the domain
- **Decidability**: Can we determine whether arbitrary statements follow from postulates?

## Philosophical Issues

### Conventional vs Objective

- **Conventionalism**: Postulates are arbitrary human conventions
- **Platonism**: Postulates describe objective mathematical reality
- **Formalism**: Postulates are rules for manipulating symbols
- **Intuitionism**: Postulates must be constructively justifiable

### Truth and Interpretation

- **Uninterpreted systems**: Postulates as formal rules without meaning
- **Interpreted systems**: Postulates make claims about specific domains
- **Multiple interpretations**: Same formal system may describe different realities
- **Semantic vs syntactic**: Meaning vs proof-theoretic role

### Revision and Choice

- **Historical revision**: Postulates changed as mathematics develops
- **Pragmatic considerations**: Usefulness, simplicity, fruitfulness
- **Theoretical virtues**: Elegance, unity, explanatory power
- **Empirical constraints**: Physical applications may guide choice

## Contemporary Developments

### Computer Science

- **Formal specifications**: Postulates defining software behavior
- **Programming axioms**: Basic assumptions about computational processes
- **Verification**: Proving programs satisfy their postulates
- **Type theory**: Postulates about data types and functions

### Logic and Foundations

- **Modal logic**: Postulates about necessity and possibility
- **Temporal logic**: Postulates about time and change
- **Deontic logic**: Postulates about obligation and permission
- **Relevant logic**: Postulates restricting [valid inference](inference.md)

### Philosophy of Science

- **Scientific postulates**: Basic assumptions of scientific theories
- **Bridge principles**: Connecting theoretical and observational terms
- **Correspondence rules**: Relating mathematical formalism to empirical phenomena
- **Paradigm shifts**: Changing fundamental postulates in science

## Relationship to Other Concepts

### [First Principles](../meta-philosophy/first-principles.md)

- **Starting points**: Both serve as foundations for reasoning
- **Epistemic status**: First principles may claim stronger justification
- **Scope**: First principles broader than mathematical postulates
- **Method**: Both enable reasoning "a fortiori" from basics to conclusions

### [Foundations](../meta-philosophy/foundation.md)

- **Foundational role**: Postulates provide foundations for mathematical systems
- **Justification**: May not require external justification within system
- **Systematic function**: Enable construction of complex theories
- **Regress stopping**: Prevent infinite regress of justification

### [Deduction](deduction.md)

- **Premise function**: Postulates serve as premises for deductive arguments
- **Validity**: Postulates don't affect validity, only [soundness](soundness.md)
- **Systematic derivation**: Enable step-by-step construction of theorems
- **Logical consequence**: Theorems follow necessarily from postulates

## Applications

### Mathematics

- **Algebra**: Group axioms, field axioms, ring axioms
- **Analysis**: Real number axioms, continuity postulates
- **Topology**: Axioms for open sets, neighborhoods, convergence
- **Probability**: Kolmogorov axioms for probability measures

### Physics

- **Mechanics**: Newton's laws as postulates of classical mechanics
- **Thermodynamics**: Laws of thermodynamics as foundational postulates
- **Relativity**: Postulates of special and general relativity
- **Quantum mechanics**: Born postulates, measurement postulates

### Philosophy

- **Ethical systems**: Basic moral principles as postulates
- **Epistemology**: Foundational assumptions about knowledge
- **Metaphysics**: Basic categories and principles of being
- **Logic**: [Classical logical axioms](classical-logical-axioms.md) as postulates of reasoning

## Significance for A Fortiori

Understanding postulates illuminates how mathematical and scientific systems are built from basic assumptions.

## Key Questions

- What makes some postulates more reasonable than others?
- Can we justify our choice of postulates or are they arbitrary?

- What is the relationship between logical axioms and mathematical postulates?
