# Religion

**Category:** philosophy-religion

## Definition
Religion is a complex cultural phenomenon typically involving beliefs about ultimate reality, practices of worship or devotion, moral codes, and organized communities of believers.

## Description
Philosophy of religion examines religious beliefs, practices, and experiences using philosophical methods. Rather than advocating for any particular religion, it analyzes concepts like God, [faith](faith.md), revelation, miracles, and religious experience to understand their logical structure and epistemic status.

## Core Elements of Religion

### Beliefs (Doctrinal Dimension)
- **Cosmological beliefs**: Origin and structure of universe
- **Theological beliefs**: Nature of divine being(s)
- **Anthropological beliefs**: Human nature, purpose, destiny
- **Eschatological beliefs**: Ultimate fate of individuals and cosmos

### Practices (Ritual Dimension)
- **Worship**: Prayer, meditation, liturgy
- **Sacraments**: Baptism, communion, initiation rites
- **Pilgrimage**: Sacred journeys and spaces
- **Festivals**: Celebration of sacred times and events

### Experience (Experiential Dimension)
- **Mystical experience**: Direct encounter with the divine
- **Conversion**: Radical transformation of life orientation
- **Prayer experience**: Sense of divine presence or communication
- **Religious emotions**: Awe, gratitude, guilt, peace

### Ethics (Moral Dimension)
- **Divine command theory**: Morality based on God's will
- **Natural law**: Moral principles discoverable by reason
- **Virtue ethics**: Character formation through religious practice
- **Social justice**: Religious obligation to help others

### Community (Social Dimension)
- **Religious institutions**: Churches, temples, sanghas
- **Religious authority**: Clergy, scriptures, tradition
- **Religious identity**: Membership in faith community
- **Transmission**: Teaching religion to next generation

## Major Philosophical Questions

### Existence of God

#### Arguments for God's Existence
- **Cosmological argument**: First cause, contingency, kalam
- **Teleological argument**: Design, fine-tuning
- **Ontological argument**: God's existence follows from concept
- **Moral argument**: Objective morality requires divine foundation
- **Argument from religious experience**: Personal encounters with divine

#### Arguments against God's Existence
- **Problem of evil**: Incompatibility of evil with perfect God
- **Argument from hiddenness**: God's existence should be more obvious
- **Argument from religious disagreement**: Multiple conflicting religions
- **Incoherence arguments**: Divine attributes are contradictory

### Problem of Evil
**Classic formulation**: If God is omnipotent, omniscient, and perfectly good, why does evil exist?

#### Theodicies (Defenses of God's Goodness)
- **Free will defense**: Evil results from human misuse of freedom
- **Soul-making theodicy**: Evil serves spiritual development
- **Greater good defense**: Evil necessary for greater goods
- **Mystery response**: Human reason too limited to understand

#### Critiques
- **Evidential problem**: Amount and distribution of evil
- **Natural evil**: Earthquakes, disease not caused by human choice
- **Animal suffering**: Innocent creatures suffer without moral benefit

### Religious Knowledge and Faith
- **Can reason prove** religious truths? ([Natural theology](faith.md))
- **Role of [faith](faith.md)** vs evidence in religious belief
- **Religious experience** as source of knowledge
- **Scripture and revelation** as epistemic sources

### Religious Language
- **Cognitive vs non-cognitive**: Do religious statements assert facts?
- **Literal vs metaphorical**: How should religious language be interpreted?
- **Verification principle**: Are religious statements meaningful?
- **Analogical predication**: How can human language apply to God?

## Diversity of Religions

### Theistic Religions
- **Monotheism**: Judaism, Christianity, Islam
- **Polytheism**: Hinduism (in some forms), ancient Greek religion
- **Henotheism**: Primary deity with others subordinate
- **Deism**: God creates but doesn't intervene

### Non-Theistic Religions
- **Buddhism**: Focus on liberation from suffering (some forms)
- **Jainism**: Emphasis on non-violence and soul purification
- **Confucianism**: Social harmony and ethical cultivation
- **Taoism**: Harmony with natural order (Tao)

### Indigenous Religions
- **Animism**: Spirits in natural objects
- **Shamanism**: Specialists mediate between worlds
- **Ancestor veneration**: Continued relationship with dead
- **Totemic systems**: Identification with animal or plant species

## Religious Pluralism

### Exclusivism
- **Only one religion** provides path to salvation/liberation
- **Other religions** are false or incomplete
- **Strong claim**: Propositional truth is exclusive
- **Example**: "No one comes to the Father except through me"

### Inclusivism
- **One religion** is fully true but others contain partial truth
- **Anonymous Christians** (Rahner): Saved through Christ unknowingly
- **Fulfillment theology**: Other religions fulfilled in true religion
- **Hierarchical approach**: Degrees of religious truth

### Pluralism
- **Multiple religions** equally valid paths to same ultimate reality
- **Hick**: Religions are culturally conditioned responses to the Real
- **Ineffability**: Ultimate reality transcends all particular descriptions
- **Transformation criterion**: Religions judged by ethical fruits

### Relativism
- **Truth relative** to particular religious communities
- **No objective religious truth**: Each tradition valid within its context
- **Cultural determinism**: Religious beliefs shaped by social factors
- **Problem**: Self-refutation and moral relativism

## Challenges to Religion

### Scientific Challenge
- **Evolutionary explanation** of religious belief
- **Cognitive science**: Religious beliefs as byproducts of mental modules
- **Neuroscience**: Religious experience correlated with brain states
- **Historical criticism**: Scientific methods applied to religious texts

### Sociological Challenge
- **Marx**: Religion as "opium of the people"
- **Durkheim**: Religion as social cohesion mechanism
- **Weber**: Religion and social change
- **Secularization thesis**: Religion declining in modern societies

### Philosophical Challenge
- **Logical positivism**: Religious statements meaningless
- **Existentialism**: Authentic existence requires abandoning illusions
- **Postmodernism**: Religious truth claims as power structures
- **Naturalism**: No supernatural entities or forces

## Contemporary Issues

### Religion and Politics
- **Separation of church and state**: Institutional boundaries
- **Religious freedom**: Individual and corporate liberty
- **Public reason**: Role of religious arguments in democracy
- **Religious fundamentalism**: Literalism and political activism

### Religion and Ethics
- **Divine command theory**: Euthyphro dilemma
- **Religious pluralism and morality**: Multiple ethical systems
- **Bioethics**: Religious perspectives on medical decisions
- **Environmental ethics**: Stewardship vs dominion

### Religion and Gender
- **Feminist theology**: Male-dominated religious traditions
- **Women's ordination**: Gender and religious authority
- **Religious law**: Treatment of women in religious legal systems
- **Goddess spirituality**: Alternative to patriarchal religions

### Religion and Science
- **Creationism vs evolution**: Literal vs metaphorical reading
- **Intelligent design**: Scientific vs religious explanation
- **Methodological naturalism**: Proper scope of scientific inquiry
- **Religious responses to cosmology**: Big Bang, multiverse theories
