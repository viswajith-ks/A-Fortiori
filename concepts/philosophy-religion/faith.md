# Faith

**Category:** philosophy-religion

## Definition
Faith is belief or trust in something that cannot be fully proven through empirical evidence or logical demonstration, often involving commitment despite uncertainty.

## Description
Faith occupies a complex position in philosophy and religion. Is faith opposed to reason, complementary to it, or a form of knowledge itself? The relationship between [faith](faith.md), [knowledge](../epistemology/knowledge.md), and [belief](../epistemology/belief.md) has been central to philosophy of religion and epistemology for centuries.

## Types of Faith

### Religious Faith
- **Trust in divine beings** or sacred teachings
- **Commitment to religious practices** and communities
- **Hope in ultimate meaning** and salvation
- **Examples**: Christian faith in God, Buddhist faith in the Three Jewels

### Propositional Faith
- **Belief that** certain statements are true
- **Faith that God exists**, miracles occur, afterlife awaits
- **Cognitive component**: Accepting propositions as true
- **Often contrasted with knowledge**: Faith begins where proof ends

### Fiduciary Faith
- **Trust in** or reliance upon someone or something
- **Personal relationship** rather than propositional belief
- **Faith in God** as trust rather than mere belief
- **Analogous to trust** in friends, institutions, or processes

### Existential Faith
- **Fundamental orientation** toward life and reality
- **Basic trust** that existence is meaningful and worthwhile
- **Kierkegaardian leap**: Passionate commitment despite uncertainty
- **Encompasses doubt**: Faith includes wrestling with questions

## Philosophical Approaches

### Faith vs Reason

#### Opposition Model
- **Tertullian**: "What has Athens to do with Jerusalem?"
- **Faith and reason** address different domains
- **Reason limited** to natural world; faith required for supernatural
- **Credo quia absurdum**: "I believe because it is absurd"

#### Harmony Model
- **Aquinas**: Faith and reason both lead to truth
- **Natural theology**: Reason can prove God's existence
- **Revealed theology**: Faith accepts what reason cannot prove
- **No genuine conflict** between properly understood faith and reason

#### Fideism
- **Faith alone** provides religious knowledge
- **Reason inadequate** or corrupting for religious truth
- **Kierkegaard**: Truth is subjectivity; objective proof misses the point
- **Plantinga**: Basic beliefs can be properly basic without evidence

### Epistemology of Faith

#### Faith as Knowledge
- **Aquinas**: Faith is a form of knowledge (though imperfect)
- **Newman**: Faith involves real assent to truth
- **Evidence**: Personal experience, testimony, natural theology
- **Certainty**: Faith can provide certainty even without proof

#### Faith as Non-Cognitive
- **Emotivism**: Faith expresses attitudes, not beliefs
- **Braithwaite**: Religious statements express moral commitment
- **Wittgensteinian**: Faith is form of life, not theoretical position
- **Pragmatism**: Faith valuable for life orientation, not truth claims

#### Faith as Reasonable Risk
- **Pascal's Wager**: Believing in God is rational bet
- **William James**: "Will to Believe" in live, forced, momentous options
- **Pragmatic benefits** of faith even if unprovable
- **Risk assessment**: Expected utility of faith vs unbelief

## Faith and Evidence

### Evidentialist Challenge
- **Clifford**: "It is wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone to believe anything upon insufficient evidence"
- **Burden of proof**: Religious claims require evidence
- **Scientific method**: Standard for all knowledge claims
- **Critique of faith**: Belief without evidence is irrational

### Reformed Epistemology Response
- **Plantinga**: Basic beliefs don't require evidence from other beliefs
- **Sensus divinitatis**: Natural capacity for knowledge of God
- **Properly basic**: Some beliefs (including God's existence) can be foundational
- **De jure vs de facto**: Faith may be warranted even if false

### Natural Theology
- **Arguments for God's existence** provide evidence for faith
- **Cosmological argument**: First cause, contingency
- **Teleological argument**: Design in nature
- **Ontological argument**: God's existence follows from concept
- **Moral argument**: Objective morality requires divine foundation

## Faith and Doubt

### Cartesian Doubt
- **[Methodological doubt](../meta-philosophy/methodological-doubt.md)** as preparation for faith
- **Clear and distinct ideas** as criteria for truth
- **God as guarantor** of reliable cognition
- **Faith built on** rational foundations

### Existential Doubt
- **Kierkegaard**: Faith requires leap over objective uncertainty
- **Anxiety and despair** as preconditions for authentic faith
- **Doubt not opposite** of faith but element within it
- **Passionate commitment** despite lack of proof

### Varieties of Doubt
- **Intellectual doubt**: Questioning truth claims
- **Emotional doubt**: Feeling abandoned or uncertain
- **Volitional doubt**: Questioning commitment and trust
- **Methodological doubt**: Tool for discovering certainty

## Contemporary Issues

### Religious Pluralism
- **Multiple conflicting faiths**: How to adjudicate truth claims?
- **Exclusivism**: Only one religion true
- **Inclusivism**: Other religions contain partial truth
- **Pluralism**: Multiple paths to same ultimate reality

### Faith and Science
- **Galileo affair**: Apparent conflict between scientific and religious truth
- **Evolution**: Challenge to literal interpretation of creation accounts
- **Methodological naturalism**: Science operates without supernatural hypotheses
- **NOMA**: Non-overlapping magisteria (Gould)

### Psychology of Faith
- **Cognitive biases**: Confirmation bias, wishful thinking
- **Social psychology**: Conformity, authority, group dynamics
- **Developmental psychology**: Stages of faith development (Fowler)
- **Neuropsychology**: Brain correlates of religious experience

## Faith in Secular Contexts

### Scientific Faith
- **Trust in scientific method** and institutions
- **Background assumptions**: Uniformity of nature, reliability of instruments
- **Peer review system**: Trust in scientific community
- **Paradigm shifts**: Require faith in new approaches

### Moral Faith
- **Trust in moral principles** despite uncertainty about foundations
- **Faith in human dignity** and rights
- **Hope for moral progress** despite evidence of evil
- **Commitment to justice** without guarantee of success

### Interpersonal Faith
- **Trust in relationships** despite vulnerability
- **Faith in others' sincerity** and good intentions
- **Commitment in marriage**: "For better or worse"
- **Social institutions**: Faith in democracy, legal system
