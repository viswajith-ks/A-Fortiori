# Reduction

**Category:** meta-philosophy

## Definition

Reduction is the philosophical method of explaining complex phenomena by analyzing them into simpler components or more fundamental entities.

## Major Types

- **Ontological Reduction:** Explaining things by their underlying substances or elements (e.g., water as H₂O).
- **Epistemological Reduction:** Explaining complex knowledge systems with simpler foundational rules.
- **Methodological Reduction:** Approach used in science and analytic philosophy to clarify concepts.

## Strengths

- Allows for unified explanations.
- Supports the search for [foundations](foundation.md) and [first principles](first-principles.md).
- Has led to tremendous scientific progress.

## Criticisms

- May ignore emergent properties or relationships that cannot be explained solely by parts.
- Risks oversimplifying complex systems, especially in biology, psychology, or social sciences.
- Anti-reductionism: Some phenomena are “irreducible”—consciousness, moral value, mathematical truths, etc.

## Connections

- Reduction vs. holism (treating systems as integrated wholes).
- Debates in science, philosophy of mind, and metaphysics.
