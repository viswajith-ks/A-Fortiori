# Methodological Doubt

**Category:** meta-philosophy

## Definition

Methodological doubt is a systematic process of suspending or questioning all
beliefs that can possibly be doubted, pursued to discover indubitable
foundations of knowledge.

## Origin

Developed by René Descartes in his _Meditations on First Philosophy_, this
method became central to modern epistemology.

## Steps

1. Doubt all beliefs—even those taken for granted (perception, tradition).
2. Reject beliefs that cannot withstand radical skepticism.
3. Accept only those beliefs that remain absolutely certain (_cogito ergo sum_:
   “I think, therefore I am”).

## Philosophical Purpose

- Eliminate dogma and inherited error.
- Find secure starting points for science and philosophy
  ([foundation](foundation.md)).
- Test what truly counts as knowledge
  ([certainty](../epistemology/certainty.md)).

## Critiques

- May lead to extreme skepticism.
- Some argue it's impossible to doubt all beliefs at once.
- Anti-foundationalists prefer holistic or contextual approaches.

## Applications

- Scientific methodology (rigorous hypothesis testing).
- Philosophical analysis (clarifying assumptions).
- Everyday critical thinking (spotting bias).
