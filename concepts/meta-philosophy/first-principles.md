# First Principles

**Category:** meta-philosophy

## Definition
First principles are basic propositions or assumptions that cannot be deduced from any other proposition or assumption within a given domain of inquiry.

## Description
First principles thinking, originating with Aristotle, involves breaking down complex problems to their fundamental elements—the irreducible truths that serve as the foundation for all other knowledge in that domain. This method requires abandoning assumptions and reasoning upward from foundational truths.

According to Aristotle's *Metaphysics*, first principles are "the first basis from which a thing is known" and represent the necessary conditions for the existence and understanding of something. They are not merely logical constructs but the actual foundations of reality and knowledge.

## Key Features
- **Irreducible**: Cannot be broken down further within the system
- **Foundational**: Everything else builds upon them
- **Context-specific**: Different domains have different first principles
- **Epistemically prior**: Must be known before other truths can be established

## Aristotle's Four Causes (Types of First Principles)
1. **Material Cause**: What something is made of
2. **Formal Cause**: The structure or essence that defines it
3. **Efficient Cause**: What brings it into being
4. **Final Cause**: The purpose or end toward which it aims

## First Principles Thinking Process
1. Identify and question all assumptions
2. Break down the problem to fundamental elements
3. Create new solutions from these foundations
4. Reason upward without inherited biases
