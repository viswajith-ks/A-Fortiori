# Foundation

**Category:** meta-philosophy

## Definition

A foundation in philosophy is a basic, self-evident, or indubitable starting point that supports all other knowledge or belief within a system.

## Description

The search for foundations drives much of philosophical inquiry. What can we know with absolute certainty? What assumptions are unavoidable? Different philosophical traditions propose different foundations—from Descartes' *cogito* to empiricist sense-data to pragmatic success.

## Types of Foundations

### Epistemological Foundations

Basic beliefs that require no further [justification](../epistemology/justification.md):

- **Cartesian**: Clear and distinct ideas (*cogito ergo sum*)
- **Empiricist**: Sense experience and observation
- **Rationalist**: Self-evident truths of reason

### Logical Foundations  

[Axioms](../logic/axiom.md) and [first principles](first-principles.md) that ground reasoning:

- **Classical**: Laws of logic ([Classical Logical Axioms](../logic/classical-logical-axioms.md))
- **Mathematical**: Set theory, number theory axioms
- **Conceptual**: Definitions and meaning relationships

### Metaphysical Foundations

Basic categories of existence and reality:

- **Substance**: What exists fundamentally
- **Properties**: How things can be characterized
- **Relations**: How things connect to each other

## Foundationalism vs Anti-Foundationalism

### Foundationalist Position

- Knowledge requires ultimate foundations
- Infinite regress must stop somewhere
- Some beliefs are self-justifying or indubitable

### Anti-Foundationalist Critiques

- **Coherentism**: Beliefs justified by mutual support, not foundations
- **Contextualism**: Standards vary by situation, no absolute foundations
- **Pragmatism**: Usefulness matters more than foundational certainty

## Problems with Foundations

### The Regress Problem

Every justification seems to require further justification:

1. **Infinite regress**: Justification goes on forever
2. **Circular reasoning**: Eventually refers back to what needs justification  
3. **Arbitrary stopping**: Accepts some claims without justification

### The Myth of the Given

Can we have genuinely "foundational" experiences that require no interpretation or conceptualization?

### Cultural Relativism

Are foundations universal or do they vary across cultures and historical periods?

## A Fortiori and Foundations

This project embodies foundationalist methodology:

- Start with most basic concepts ([axioms](../logic/axiom.md), [first principles](first-principles.md))
- Build systematically toward more complex ideas
- Maintain logical connections throughout

## Contemporary Approaches

- **Modest Foundationalism**: Foundations need not be infallible, just sufficiently secure
- **Reformed Epistemology**: Basic beliefs can be properly basic in appropriate circumstances
- **Naturalized Epistemology**: Use empirical methods to study knowledge formation
