# Aristotelian Causation

**Category:** meta-philosophy

## Definition

Aristotelian causation is the theory of four types of causes that explain why things exist and change: material, formal, efficient, and final causes.

## Description

[Aristotle](../../thinkers/aristotle.md) rejected the idea that "cause" means only efficient causation (what we usually call "cause" today). He argued that complete explanation requires understanding four different aspects of causation. This framework dominated Western thought for over 2000 years.

## The Four Causes

### Material Cause (*causa materialis*)

#### What something is made of

- Example: The bronze of a statue, the wood of a table
- The substance or matter that constitutes an object
- Provides the "stuff" but not the organization

### Formal Cause (*causa formalis*)  

#### The structure, pattern, or essence that defines something

- Example: The shape that makes bronze into a statue of Apollo
- The organizing principle that makes matter into a specific thing
- Often identified with the "essence" or "whatness" of something

### Efficient Cause (*causa efficiens*)

#### What brings something into being or causes change

- Example: The sculptor who shapes the bronze, the carpenter who builds the table
- Closest to modern notion of "cause"
- The agent or process that produces change

### Final Cause (*causa finalis*)

#### The purpose, goal, or end toward which something aims

- Example: The purpose of the statue (honor Apollo), the function of the table (hold objects)
- The "that for the sake of which" something exists or acts
- Most controversial in modern science

## Philosophical Significance

### Complete Explanation

Aristotle argued that understanding anything fully requires all four causes:

- A statue is bronze (material) shaped as Apollo (formal) by a sculptor (efficient) to honor the god (final)

### Teleological Worldview

Final causes suggest the universe is purposeful rather than merely mechanical—everything aims toward some good or end.

### Scientific Method

Modern science largely abandoned formal and final causes, focusing on efficient causation and mathematical description.

## Contemporary Relevance

### Biology and Function

Final cause thinking persists in biology:

- "The heart exists in order to pump blood"
- Function and purpose seem central to biological explanation

### Philosophy of Mind

Intentionality and purpose appear central to mental phenomena—minds seem to be "about" or "directed toward" things.

### Ethics and Politics  

Aristotle's ethics depends on final causation—human flourishing (*eudaimonia*) as the final cause of human action.

## Criticisms and Defenses

### Modern Scientific Criticism

- Science succeeds without final causes
- Apparent purpose explained by natural selection
- Efficient causes sufficient for explanation

### Contemporary Defenses

- Emergence and systems theory suggest formal causation
- Functions seem irreducible in biology and psychology
- Mathematical structures play causal roles

## Applications in A Fortiori

Understanding Aristotelian causation helps evaluate:

- What kinds of explanation are satisfactory
- How to analyze complex philosophical arguments
- The relationship between description and purpose
