# Reality

**Category:** metaphysics

## Definition

Reality is the totality of what exists, whether observable or unobservable, material or immaterial, actual or necessary.

## Description

The nature of reality is perhaps the most fundamental philosophical question. What exists? What is the relationship between appearance and reality? Is reality mind-independent or does consciousness play a constitutive role? These questions connect metaphysics to [epistemology](../epistemology/knowledge.md), [logic](../logic/logic.md), and science.

## Major Positions

### Materialism/Physicalism

- **Core claim**: Only physical entities exist
- **Everything** reducible to matter, energy, and physical forces
- **Implications**: Mental states are brain states; abstract objects are constructions
- **Challenges**: Consciousness, mathematical objects, moral facts

### Idealism

- **Core claim**: Reality is fundamentally mental or spiritual
- **Berkeley**: "To be is to be perceived" (*esse est percipi*)
- **Absolute idealism**: Reality as unified conscious experience
- **Challenges**: External world, scientific realism, intersubjective agreement

### Dualism

- **Core claim**: Both material and mental substances exist
- **Cartesian dualism**: Mind and body as distinct substances
- **Property dualism**: One substance with mental and physical properties
- **Challenges**: Mind-body interaction, causal closure

### Neutral Monism

- **Core claim**: Reality consists of one fundamental substance that is neither mental nor physical
- **Russell, James**: Experience as fundamental building block
- **Modern versions**: Information, process, or experiential monism

## Levels of Reality

### Fundamental vs Emergent

- **Fundamental**: Basic constituents (particles, fields, consciousness)
- **Emergent**: Higher-level patterns (life, mind, institutions)
- **Question**: Are emergent properties genuinely real or just useful descriptions?

### Abstract vs Concrete

- **Concrete entities**: Spatiotemporal objects and events
- **Abstract entities**: Numbers, properties, propositions, possible worlds
- **Platonism**: Abstract objects exist independently
- **Nominalism**: Only concrete particulars exist

## Reality and Knowledge

### Realism vs Anti-Realism

- **Realism**: Reality exists independently of our knowledge or concepts
- **Anti-realism**: Reality is partially constituted by our conceptual schemes
- **Critical realism**: Reality exists but is only accessible through our concepts

### Appearance vs Reality

- **Distinction**: How things seem vs how they actually are
- **Plato's cave**: Shadows on wall vs forms outside
- **Kant**: Phenomena (appearances) vs noumena (things-in-themselves)
- **Modern science**: Observable properties vs underlying structures

## Scientific Realism Debate

### Scientific Realism

- **Position**: Science describes reality as it actually is
- **Theoretical entities** (electrons, quarks) are real
- **Success of science** suggests truth of theories

### Anti-Realism/Instrumentalism

- **Position**: Scientific theories are useful tools, not literal descriptions
- **Underdetermination**: Multiple theories compatible with same evidence
- **History of science**: Past theories were false but useful

## Temporal Aspects of Reality

### Presentism vs Eternalism

- **Presentism**: Only the present moment exists
- **Eternalism**: Past, present, and future all equally real
- **Growing block**: Past and present exist, future doesn't

### Change and Persistence

- **How do objects persist through time?**
- **Endurantism**: Objects wholly present at each moment
- **Perdurantism**: Objects extended through time like space

## Modal Aspects of Reality

### Possible Worlds

- **Tools for analyzing** necessity, possibility, and counterfactuals
- **Modal realism** (Lewis): All possible worlds equally real
- **Moderate realism**: Possible worlds as abstract objects
- **Nominalism**: No possible worlds, just linguistic constructions

### Necessary vs Contingent

- **Necessary truths**: Could not be otherwise (logical, mathematical)
- **Contingent truths**: Could be otherwise (empirical facts)
- **Essential vs accidental properties** of objects

## Contemporary Issues

### Quantum Mechanics and Reality

- **Observer effect**: Does measurement create reality?
- **Multiple interpretations**: Copenhagen, many-worlds, hidden variables
- **Quantum entanglement**: Non-local connections

### Consciousness and Reality

- **Hard problem**: How does subjective experience arise from physical processes?
- **Panpsychism**: Consciousness as fundamental feature of reality
- **Emergentism**: Consciousness emerges from complex information processing

### Information and Reality

- **Digital physics**: Reality as computational process
- **It from bit** (Wheeler): Information as fundamental
- **Simulation hypothesis**: Are we living in a simulation?

## Significance for A Fortiori

Understanding different conceptions of reality is crucial for evaluating philosophical arguments. Claims about knowledge, logic, ethics, and religion all presuppose views about what exists and how we can know it.

## Key Questions

- What is the fundamental nature of reality?
- How does consciousness relate to physical reality?
- Can we know reality as it is in itself, or only as it appears to us?
- Are there multiple levels or kinds of reality?
