# Laws of Nature

**Category:** metaphysics

## Definition

Laws of nature are universal principles that describe the regular, predictable patterns of behavior exhibited by natural phenomena - statements expressing the fundamental regularities governing the physical universe.

## Description

Laws of nature are central to science, philosophy, and our understanding of [reality](reality.md). They connect our observations of regular patterns with deeper questions about [necessity](necessity.md) ⚠️ Placeholder, [causation](causation.md), and the structure of the natural world. The nature of natural laws - whether they describe or prescribe, whether they're discovered or constructed - remains one of philosophy's most debated topics.

## Characteristics of Natural Laws

### Universality

- **Spatial universality**: Hold everywhere in the universe
- **Temporal universality**: Hold at all times (past, present, future)
- **No exceptions**: Apply without regard to particular circumstances
- **Scope**: Cover all instances of relevant phenomena

### Necessity

- **Nomological necessity**: Could not be otherwise given natural structure
- **Physical necessity**: Distinguish from logical, metaphysical necessity
- **Counterfactual support**: Laws support counterfactual conditionals
- **Modal force**: Express what must happen, not just what does happen

### Explanatory Power

- **Subsumption**: Explain particular events by subsuming under general patterns
- **Unification**: Connect diverse phenomena under common principles
- **Prediction**: Enable reliable forecasting of future events
- **Systematicity**: Organize natural phenomena into coherent structure

### Mathematical Form

- **Quantitative precision**: Often expressible in mathematical equations
- **Functional relationships**: Describe how variables relate to each other
- **Conservation principles**: Quantities that remain constant through change
- **Symmetries**: Invariances under transformations

## Types of Natural Laws

### Fundamental vs Derivative

- **Fundamental**: Most basic principles (quantum field theory, general relativity)
- **Derivative**: Higher-level regularities emerging from fundamental laws
- **Special sciences**: Biology, chemistry, psychology may have autonomous laws
- **Reduction question**: Whether all laws reduce to physics

### Deterministic vs Statistical

- **Deterministic**: Precise specification of outcomes (classical mechanics)
- **Statistical**: Probabilistic patterns (quantum mechanics, thermodynamics)
- **Emergent determinism**: Deterministic laws at macro level from statistical micro laws
- **Fundamental randomness**: Whether indeterminacy exists at deepest level

### Conservation Laws

- **Energy conservation**: Total energy constant in isolated systems
- **Momentum conservation**: Total momentum constant without external forces
- **Charge conservation**: Electric charge neither created nor destroyed
- **Symmetry principles**: Conservation laws connected to symmetries (Noether's theorem)

### Field Equations

- **Maxwell's equations**: Electromagnetic field behavior
- **Einstein's equations**: Gravitational field and spacetime geometry
- **Schrödinger equation**: Quantum mechanical wave function evolution
- **Standard Model**: Fundamental particle interactions

## Historical Development

### Ancient and Medieval

- **Aristotelian**: Natural places and tendencies, teleological explanations
- **Stoic**: Divine logos as rational principle governing cosmos
- **Medieval**: Divine command theory - God as source of natural order
- **Natural law tradition**: Moral and physical law connected

### Scientific Revolution

- **Galileo**: Mathematical nature of natural laws ("Book of Nature written in mathematics")
- **Newton**: Universal gravitation as paradigmatic natural law
- **Mechanical philosophy**: Deterministic laws governing matter in motion
- **Uniformity**: Same laws apply to terrestrial and celestial phenomena

### Modern Physics

- **Relativity**: Laws must be invariant under coordinate transformations
- **Quantum mechanics**: Probabilistic laws, measurement problem
- **Quantum field theory**: Particle interactions through field exchanges
- **Standard Model**: Unification of electromagnetic, weak, and strong forces

### Contemporary Developments

- **Chaos theory**: Deterministic systems with unpredictable behavior
- **Complexity theory**: Emergent laws in complex systems
- **Information theory**: Laws as information processing principles
- **Multiverse theories**: Different laws in different universes?

## Philosophical Theories

### Regularity Theory (Humean)

- **Core idea**: Laws are stable, exceptionless regularities
- **No necessity**: Laws describe what happens, don't necessitate outcomes
- **Best system**: Laws are theorems in best axiomatization of all truths
- **[Hume's approach](../../thinkers/david-hume.md)**: Constant conjunction without necessary connection

**Advantages**: Empiricist, avoids mysterious necessities, fits scientific practice

**Problems**: Accidental vs lawlike regularities, counterfactuals, uninstantiated laws

### Necessitarian Theory

- **Core idea**: Laws express genuine necessities in nature
- **Natural necessity**: Stronger than regularity, weaker than logical necessity
- **Modal force**: Laws make certain outcomes necessary, others impossible
- **Realist interpretation**: Laws describe objective features of reality

**Advantages**: Explains modal force, supports scientific realism, grounds counterfactuals

**Problems**: What is natural necessity? How do we know laws are necessary?

### Dispositionalist Theory

- **Core idea**: Laws emerge from intrinsic causal powers of objects
- **Fundamental properties**: Objects have dispositional properties essentially
- **Manifestation**: Laws describe when and how dispositions manifest
- **Bottom-up**: Laws derive from nature of fundamental particles/properties

**Advantages**: Grounds laws in nature of things, explains [causation](causation.md), naturalistic

**Problems**: Circularity, what grounds dispositions? Relation to fundamental physics

### Primitivist Theory

- **Core idea**: Laws are fundamental, irreducible features of reality
- **Governing**: Laws govern events rather than describing them
- **Temporal priority**: Laws exist prior to and independently of their instances
- **Platonic**: Laws as abstract objects standing in governing relation to world

**Advantages**: Robust modal force, clear counterfactual support, fits physics practice

**Problems**: Metaphysically extravagant, how do abstract laws influence concrete events?

## Relationship to Other Concepts

### [Causation](causation.md)

- **Laws and causes**: How do natural laws relate to causal relationships?
- **Causal necessity**: Do laws ground causal necessity or vice versa?
- **Singular causation**: Laws vs individual causal relationships
- **Causal explanation**: Laws enable causal explanations of events

### [Determinism](determinism.md)

- **Laplacean determinism**: Deterministic laws plus initial conditions determine all future states
- **Causal closure**: Laws ensure every physical event has sufficient physical cause
- **Predictability**: Laws enable prediction, though not always practically
- **Quantum challenge**: Statistical laws challenge classical determinism

### Induction and Confirmation

- **[Problem of induction](../../arguments/problem-of-induction.md)**: How do we know laws will continue to hold?
- **Confirmation**: How do instances confirm general laws?
- **Curve-fitting**: Infinite laws compatible with finite evidence
- **Theory choice**: Which laws best supported by evidence?

### Explanation and Understanding

- **Deductive-nomological**: Explanation by subsuming under laws
- **Unification**: Laws provide unified understanding of diverse phenomena
- **Mechanism**: Laws vs mechanistic explanations
- **Levels**: Relationship between laws at different levels of description

## Problems and Puzzles

### Ceteris Paribus Laws

- **Problem**: Real laws seem to have exceptions
- **Example**: "Supply and demand determine price" - true other things being equal
- **Solutions**: Idealization, implicit exception clauses, statistical interpretation
- **Scope**: Are fundamental physical laws exception-free?

### Laws and Accidents

- **Distinction**: Lawlike vs accidental generalizations
- **Example**: "All gold spheres are less than one mile in diameter" - accident or law?
- **Projectability**: Which predicates support inductive inferences?
- **Natural kinds**: Connection between laws and objective classifications

### Uninstantiated Laws

- **Problem**: Laws about types of objects that don't exist
- **Example**: Laws governing tachyons, which may not exist
- **Counterfactual**: "If there were tachyons, they would travel faster than light"
- **Implications**: Laws independent of their instances?

### Laws in Special Sciences

- **Autonomy**: Do biology, psychology have their own laws?
- **Reduction**: Must all laws reduce to fundamental physics?
- **Emergence**: Can higher-level laws be irreducible?
- **Multiple realizability**: Same law realized in different physical systems

## Contemporary Debates

### Laws and Symmetries

- **Noether's theorem**: Symmetries correspond to conservation laws
- **Gauge theories**: Physical laws invariant under gauge transformations
- **Spontaneous symmetry breaking**: Laws with less symmetry than underlying theory
- **Fundamental role**: Are symmetries more basic than laws?

### Laws and Information

- **Information theory**: Physical laws as information processing principles
- **Computational universe**: Reality as computational process following algorithmic rules
- **Digital physics**: Laws as rules of cellular automaton
- **It from bit**: Information as more fundamental than matter/energy

### Laws and Time

- **Time-reversal symmetry**: Most fundamental laws work equally well forward and backward in time
- **Thermodynamic arrow**: Statistical laws provide temporal direction
- **Block universe**: All times equally real, laws describe 4-dimensional structure
- **Temporal becoming**: Laws governing genuine temporal flow

### Laws and Modality

- **Possible worlds**: Laws true in all nomologically possible worlds
- **Natural properties**: Laws involve only natural (not gruesome) properties
- **Alien properties**: Could there be laws involving properties not instantiated in our world?
- **Modal realism**: What would laws be like in other possible worlds?

## Scientific Applications

### Theory Construction

- **Empirical adequacy**: Laws must fit observational data
- **Simplicity**: Prefer simpler laws other things being equal
- **Unification**: Laws that connect previously separate phenomena
- **Fertility**: Laws suggesting new research programs

### Experimental Design

- **Controlled conditions**: Create situations where laws can be tested
- **Idealization**: Abstract from complicating factors
- **Repeatability**: Laws must be testable under reproducible conditions
- **Statistical analysis**: Methods for detecting lawlike patterns

### Technology and Engineering

- **Reliability**: Technology depends on laws being stable and universal
- **Design principles**: Engineer systems exploiting natural laws
- **Prediction**: Use laws to predict behavior of engineered systems
- **Control**: Manipulate conditions to achieve desired outcomes

## Connection to [Hume's Miracles Argument](../../arguments/hume-miracles.md)

### Laws as Evidence Against Miracles

- **Uniform experience**: Laws established by universal human experience
- **Violation**: Miracles by definition violate natural laws
- **Evidence balance**: Testimony for miracle vs evidence for law
- **Extraordinary claims**: More extraordinary the violation, stronger evidence needed

### Responses to Hume

- **Laws vs regularities**: If laws merely describe, violations possible
- **Divine action**: God as author of laws could suspend them
- **Higher laws**: Miracles might follow spiritual laws unknown to science
- **Statistical laws**: Quantum mechanics allows genuine randomness
