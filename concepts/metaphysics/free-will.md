# Free Will

**Category:** metaphysics

## Definition

Free will is the capacity of agents to choose between genuine alternatives - the power to have acted differently in identical circumstances, making agents the ultimate source and author of their actions.

## Description

Free will is one of philosophy's most enduring and practical problems. It concerns whether humans possess genuine agency - the ability to make choices that are truly "up to them" in a way that grounds moral responsibility. The question intersects with [determinism](determinism.md), neuroscience, ethics, law, and our basic understanding of human nature.

## Core Components

### Alternative Possibilities

- **Could have done otherwise**: In identical circumstances, agent could have chosen differently
- **Genuine choice**: Multiple options truly available to agent
- **Real alternatives**: Not merely epistemic - actually different paths possible
- **Frankfurt cases**: Challenges to necessity of alternative possibilities

### Ultimate Origination

- **Agent as source**: Actions originate with agent, not merely passed through
- **Ultimate responsibility**: Agent bears final responsibility for action
- **Causal chain**: Agent initiates causal sequence, doesn't merely continue it
- **Self-determination**: Agent determines their own character and choices

### Control and Agency

- **Rational control**: Ability to act on reasons and deliberate
- **Self-control**: Power to resist impulses and unwanted desires
- **Moral agency**: Capacity for moral evaluation and response
- **Personal autonomy**: Self-governance and self-direction

## Major Positions

### Libertarianism

**Thesis**: Free will exists and is incompatible with [determinism](determinism.md)

**Requirements**:

- **Indeterminism**: Some events genuinely uncaused or undetermined
- **Agent causation**: Agents as uncaused causers of their actions
- **Ultimate origination**: Actions originate with agent, not prior causes
- **Alternative possibilities**: Could genuinely have done otherwise

**Advocates**: William James, C.A. Campbell, Robert Kane, Timothy O'Connor

**Problems**:

- **Randomness objection**: How can random events ground free will?
- **Explanation gap**: How does agent causation work?
- **Scientific challenge**: Evidence for universal [causation](causation.md)

### Hard Determinism

**Thesis**: [Determinism](determinism.md) is true and incompatible with free will

**Conclusion**: Free will is illusion; no ultimate moral responsibility

**Arguments**:

- **Causal closure**: All physical events have sufficient physical causes
- **Scientific success**: Deterministic theories highly successful
- **Predictability**: Human behavior often predictable
- **No gaps**: No room for uncaused mental causation

**Advocates**: Paul Holbach, Arthur Schopenhauer, Paul Halbach

**Problems**:

- **Quantum mechanics**: Apparent indeterminacy at fundamental level
- **Practical consequences**: Undermines moral and legal practices
- **Self-refutation**: If belief determined, no reason to accept argument
- **Phenomenology**: Conflicts with experience of choice

### Compatibilism (Soft Determinism)

**Thesis**: Free will is compatible with [determinism](determinism.md)

**Strategy**: Redefine free will in terms compatible with [causation](causation.md)

**Conditions for Free Action**:

- **Voluntary**: Action flows from agent's desires and decisions
- **Uncoerced**: Not forced by external threats or manipulation
- **Rational**: Based on reasons, reflection, and deliberation
- **Expresses agency**: Action authentic to agent's character and values

**Classical Compatibilists**: Hobbes, Hume, Mill  
**Contemporary**: Harry Frankfurt, Susan Wolf, John Martin Fischer

### Hard Incompatibilism

**Thesis**: Free will incompatible with both determinism and indeterminism

**Conclusion**: Free will doesn't exist, but life can still be meaningful

**Argument**:

- **Determinism**: No alternative possibilities, no ultimate origination
- **Indeterminism**: Actions random, not genuinely agent-caused
- **Dilemma**: Either determined (not free) or random (not controlled)

**Advocate**: Derk Pereboom

**Response**: Accept absence of ultimate responsibility while maintaining practical attitudes and meaning

## Arguments Against Free Will

### The Consequence Argument

1. **No control over past**: Cannot change what happened before birth
2. **No control over laws**: Cannot change [laws of nature](laws-of-nature.md)
3. **Past + laws determine present**: Current state fixed by past and laws
4. **Therefore**: No control over current state - no free will

**Responses**: Deny transfer of powerlessness; distinguish types of control; question fixity of past

### Manipulation Arguments

1. **Manipulation cases**: Agent's desires shaped by external manipulation
2. **Causal history**: All desires have causal history stretching to factors beyond control
3. **No relevant difference**: Gradual determination vs sudden manipulation
4. **Therefore**: No actions truly free from external determination

**Responses**: Distinguish proximate from remote causes; emphasize agent's current capacities; historical vs ahistorical approaches

### Neuroscience Arguments

**Libet Experiments**:

- **Readiness potential**: Brain activity precedes conscious intention by 500ms
- **Interpretation**: Unconscious brain processes initiate action
- **Veto power**: Consciousness can still stop action
- **Implication**: Actions begin unconsciously, limiting free will

**Responses**: Question interpretation; distinguish initiation from control; emphasize complexity of real decisions

### Argument from Luck

1. **Ultimate origination**: Free will requires agent be ultimate source
2. **Regress**: Agent's character shaped by factors beyond control
3. **Present randomness**: Random events don't help - still not controlled
4. **Therefore**: Actions either determined by factors beyond control or random

**Responses**: Deny need for ultimate origination; develop notion of degrees; emphasize self-modification over time

## Arguments for Free Will

### Phenomenological Argument

- **Direct experience**: We experience ourselves as choosing
- **Deliberation**: Feel weight of different options
- **Effort**: Experience of trying and self-control
- **Regret**: Sense that we could have chosen differently

**Responses**: Experiences might be illusions; phenomenology not reliable guide to metaphysics

### Moral Responsibility Argument

- **Moral practices**: Praise, blame, punishment presuppose free will
- **Reactive attitudes**: Resentment, gratitude imply free choice
- **Justice**: Criminal law assumes responsibility for actions
- **Personal relationships**: Love, friendship assume genuine agency

**Responses**: Practices might be based on false beliefs; could revise practices; forward-looking vs backward-looking responsibility

### Argument from Quantum Mechanics

- **Indeterminacy**: Fundamental randomness at quantum level
- **Amplification**: Quantum events can affect macro world
- **Neural quantum effects**: Brain might exploit quantum indeterminacy
- **Agent influence**: Consciousness might bias quantum outcomes

**Responses**: Randomness doesn't equal free will; no evidence for quantum effects in brain; amplification mechanisms unclear

### Transcendental Argument

- **Conditions of morality**: Moral evaluation requires free will
- **Conditions of rationality**: Reasoning presupposes freedom of thought
- **Conditions of agency**: Action concept requires free choice
- **Therefore**: Free will necessary for human practices

**Responses**: Practices might not require ultimate free will; could be based on degrees of freedom; naturalistic explanations possible

## Contemporary Approaches

### Degrees of Freedom

- **Not all-or-nothing**: Freedom comes in degrees
- **Development**: Agents acquire more freedom over time
- **Context-dependent**: Freedom varies by situation and domain
- **Practical focus**: Emphasize enhancing rather than perfect freedom

### Mesh Theories

- **Historical**: What matters is how action fits agent's history
- **Reasons-responsiveness**: Capacity to respond appropriately to reasons
- **Self-expression**: Action expresses agent's deep values and commitments
- **Authenticity**: Action flows from agent's true self

### Semi-compatibilism

- **Moral responsibility**: Compatible with determinism
- **Alternative possibilities**: Not necessary for responsibility
- **Guidance control**: Agent exercises appropriate control over action
- **Frankfurt cases**: Show alternative possibilities not required

### Event-causal Libertarianism

- **Quantum amplification**: Quantum indeterminacy affects brain states
- **Self-forming actions**: Agents shape their own character through choices
- **Probabilistic causation**: Actions influenced but not determined by reasons
- **Gradual formation**: Free will develops through repeated exercises
