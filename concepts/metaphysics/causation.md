# Causation

**Category:** metaphysics

## Definition
Causation is the relationship between events where one event (the cause) brings about, produces, or determines another event (the effect).

## Description
Causation is one of the most fundamental concepts in metaphysics, science, and everyday reasoning. We constantly think in terms of causes and effects: diseases cause symptoms, desires cause actions, forces cause motion. Yet the precise nature of causal relationships has puzzled philosophers since ancient times and remains actively debated today.

## Historical Approaches

### [Aristotelian Causation](../meta-philosophy/aristotelian-causation.md)
**Four Types of Causes:**
- **Material cause**: What something is made of
- **Formal cause**: The structure or pattern that makes it what it is  
- **Efficient cause**: The agent or process that brings about change
- **Final cause**: The purpose or goal toward which something aims

**Modern focus**: Primarily on efficient causation, though other types remain relevant in biology and psychology.

### Scholastic Developments
- **Principal vs instrumental causes**: Primary agents vs secondary tools
- **Universal vs particular causes**: General causal powers vs specific instances
- **Proximate vs remote causes**: Immediate vs distant causal factors
- **Necessary vs contingent causation**: Required vs optional causal relationships

## Modern Philosophical Theories

### Humean Regularity Theory
**[David Hume's](../thinkers/david-hume.md) ⚠️ Placeholder Analysis:**
- **Constant conjunction**: A-type events regularly followed by B-type events
- **Temporal priority**: Causes precede effects in time
- **Contiguity**: Causes and effects connected in space and time
- **No necessary connection**: Only observed correlation, not metaphysical necessity

**Problems:**
- **Accidental regularities**: Correlation without causation
- **Spurious correlation**: Common causes creating false connections
- **Single case causation**: Individual causal claims not covered by regularities

### Counterfactual Theory
**Basic Idea**: A causes B if B would not have occurred without A

**Lewis's Analysis:**
- **Closest possible worlds**: Compare actual world to most similar worlds where cause absent
- **Causal dependence**: Effect depends on cause across possible worlds
- **Causal chains**: Transitive chains of counterfactual dependence

**Advantages:**
- **Handles single cases**: Each instance evaluated separately
- **Intuitive**: Matches common sense causal judgments
- **Precise**: Formal apparatus using possible worlds semantics

**Problems:**
- **Overdetermination**: Multiple sufficient causes
- **Preemption**: Backup causes that would operate
- **Late cutting**: Causal chains interrupted after effect inevitable

### Probabilistic Causation
**Core Idea**: Causes raise the probability of their effects

**Suppes's Analysis:**
- P(B|A) > P(B): A raises probability of B
- A occurs before B temporally  
- No spurious correlation from common cause

**Refinements:**
- **Context dependence**: Causal relevance varies by background conditions
- **Token vs type**: Individual events vs kinds of events
- **Causal networks**: Multiple interacting probabilistic causes

### Mechanistic Theories
**Basic Idea**: Causation involves physical mechanisms connecting causes and effects

**Features:**
- **Productive processes**: Continuous physical processes transmit causal influence
- **Conserved quantities**: Energy, momentum, charge transferred from cause to effect
- **Spatiotemporal continuity**: No action at a distance
- **Physical necessity**: Grounded in laws of nature

**Applications:**
- **Physics**: Force interactions, field effects
- **Biology**: Biochemical pathways, genetic causation
- **Psychology**: Neural mechanisms underlying mental causation

## Contemporary Issues

### Mental Causation
**Problem**: How can mental states cause physical events?

**Approaches:**
- **Physicalism**: Mental states are identical to physical states
- **Property dualism**: Mental properties emerge from but supervene on physical
- **Epiphenomenalism**: Mental states caused by but don't cause physical events
- **Anomalous monism**: Mental causation through physical events under physical descriptions

### Agent Causation
**Problem**: How do agents cause their actions?

**Event causation**: Prior mental states cause actions (deterministic)  
**Agent causation**: Agents as substances cause actions (libertarian)  
**Reasons explanation**: Actions explained by beliefs and desires rather than caused  

### Causal Exclusion
**Problem**: If physical events have sufficient physical causes, how can mental/biological/social causes be real?

**Responses:**
- **Levels of description**: Different levels reveal different causal patterns
- **Multiple realizability**: Same causal role implemented differently  
- **Emergence**: Higher-level causes irreducible to lower-level
- **Interventionism**: Causes are variables that make a difference when manipulated

### Causation in Physics

#### Relativity Theory
- **No universal simultaneity**: Causal order relative to reference frame
- **Light cone structure**: Causal influence limited by speed of light
- **Block universe**: Past, present, future equally real

#### Quantum Mechanics
- **Probabilistic causation**: Quantum events have probabilistic rather than deterministic causes
- **Measurement problem**: Role of observation in causing wavefunction collapse
- **Non-locality**: Entangled particles instantaneously influence each other

#### Thermodynamics
- **Irreversibility**: Thermal processes create preferred temporal direction
- **Entropy increase**: Statistical tendency toward disorder
- **Emergence of time**: Thermodynamic arrow of time from underlying reversible dynamics

## Causal Reasoning

### Mill's Methods
**Methods for discovering causes:**
- **Agreement**: Common factor in all cases where effect occurs
- **Difference**: Factor present when effect occurs, absent when it doesn't
- **Joint method**: Combination of agreement and difference
- **Residues**: Subtract known causes to find cause of residual effect
- **Concomitant variation**: Effect varies proportionally with suspected cause

### Scientific Method
- **Controlled experiments**: Manipulate suspected causes, observe effects
- **Statistical analysis**: Control for confounding variables
- **Randomization**: Eliminate selection bias
- **Replication**: Ensure results not accidental

### Causal Inference
- **Bradford Hill criteria**: Guidelines for causal inference in epidemiology
- **Causal graphs**: Directed acyclic graphs representing causal relationships
- **Instrumental variables**: Use exogenous variation to identify causal effects
- **Natural experiments**: Exploit quasi-random variation in natural settings

## Metaphysical Questions

### Causal Realism vs Anti-Realism
- **Realism**: Causal relations exist independently of our knowledge
- **Anti-realism**: Causation is projection of mental habits onto world
- **Pragmatism**: Causal concepts useful for prediction and manipulation
- **Instrumentalism**: Causal theories are tools, not descriptions of reality

### Causal Powers
- **Dispositional properties**: Objects have intrinsic causal powers
- **Categorical base**: Powers grounded in non-dispositional properties
- **Pure powers**: Dispositions without categorical base
- **Powerful qualities**: Properties both qualitative and dispositional

### Laws of Nature
- **Humean**: Laws are stable regularities in events
- **Non-Humean**: Laws govern events and ground causation
- **Dispositionalist**: Laws emerge from intrinsic causal powers
- **Primitivist**: Laws are basic features of reality

## Applications

### Science
- **Experimental design**: Identifying causal relationships through controlled manipulation
- **Theory construction**: Laws express causal relationships between variables
- **Explanation**: Scientific explanations often causal explanations
- **Prediction**: Causal knowledge enables prediction and control

### Medicine
- **Etiology**: Identifying causes of disease
- **Treatment**: Interventions designed to cause beneficial effects
- **Prevention**: Eliminating or modifying causal factors
- **Prognosis**: Predicting causal consequences of conditions

### Law
- **Legal responsibility**: Assignment of blame requires causal connection
- **Tort law**: Liability depends on causing harm
- **Criminal law**: Actions must cause prohibited consequences
- **Evidence**: Establishing causal connections through testimony and physical evidence

### Psychology
- **Causal attribution**: How people explain events in terms of causes
- **Attribution errors**: Systematic mistakes in causal reasoning
- **Learned helplessness**: Perception of lack of causal control
- **Agency**: Sense of being cause of one's actions
