# Aristotle

**Life Span:** 384–322 BCE

**School:** Ancient Greek Philosophy, Peripatetic School

## Key Ideas
- **Logic as Tool (Organon):** Aristotle established logic as a method for acquiring knowledge, not merely a set of rules.
- **First Principles & Axioms:** Developed foundational methods for reasoning from “first principles” and axioms in philosophy and science.
- **Four Causes:** Introduced material, formal, efficient, and final causes as a framework for explanation.
- **Substance Theory:** Distinguished between substance and attributes, forming the basis of metaphysics.
- **Virtue Ethics:** Emphasized character and “human flourishing” (*eudaimonia*) as the aim of moral life.

## Major Works
- *Organon* (logical treatises including Prior Analytics)
- *Metaphysics*
- *Nicomachean Ethics*
- *Physics*
- *Politics*
- *De Anima* (On the Soul)

## Philosophical Position
Aristotle’s philosophy is marked by a commitment to empirical observation and systematic classification. He believed that knowledge should be built up from clear definitions, established axioms, and careful reasoning. Unlike Plato’s focus on transcendent Forms, Aristotle sought explanations grounded in the observable world and interconnected causes.

## Influences
**Influenced by:** Plato, Socratic tradition  
**Influenced:** Aquinas, Islamic philosophers (e.g., Avicenna, Averroes), modern logic, contemporary virtue ethics

## Context
Aristotle taught at the Lyceum in Athens, pioneering the earliest “research institute.” His influence stretches through medieval, Islamic, and modern philosophy, especially in logic, science, and virtue ethics.

## Significance
Aristotle’s systematic method provides the blueprint for analytic philosophy and underpins much of Western rational inquiry. His commitment to foundational reasoning, axiomatic systems, and practical virtue directly aligns with the goal of reasoning from established premises to stronger conclusions.
