# Transcendental Arguments

**Type:** Philosophical method / argument  
**Target:** [Logic](../concepts/logic/logic.md), [Epistemology](../epistemology/knowledge.md) ⚠️ Placeholder, [Metaphysics](../metaphysics/metaphysics.md)  
**Philosophers:** [Kant](../thinkers/kant.md), [Strawson](../thinkers/strawson.md), [Aristotle](../thinkers/aristotle.md)

## What Are Transcendental Arguments?
These are arguments that begin with an uncontroversial fact about experience or knowledge and reason backward to deduce the necessary conditions for its possibility.  
- Example: “We have coherent experiences; therefore, certain structures (e.g., time, causality) must be real or presupposed.”

## Structure
1. State an indubitable premise (“Experience is unified”).
2. Demonstrate that this requires certain background conditions (“Unity requires time and memory”).
3. Conclude: These conditions are rationally necessary or “transcendentally” real.

## Famous Examples
- **Kant:** Our experience of objects presupposes the concepts of space, time, and causality.
- **Strawson:** The concept of a person presupposes both physical and mental attributes.
- **Aristotle:** The possibility of meaningful speech presupposes the Law of Non-Contradiction.

## Uses
- Responding to skepticism: Show that doubting knowledge itself presupposes knowledge of certain logical rules.
- Clarifying metaphysical debates: Reveal what must be true if experience, science, or ethics are possible.

## Critiques
- Are transcendental arguments merely analytic (tautological) or do they reveal real metaphysical truths?
- Do they genuinely overcome skepticism or just show the preconditions of argument?
