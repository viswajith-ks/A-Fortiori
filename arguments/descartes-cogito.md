# Descartes' Cogito Ergo Sum

**Type:** Foundational argument (response to skepticism)  
**Target:** Establishing indubitable [foundation](../concepts/meta-philosophy/foundation.md) for [knowledge](../concepts/epistemology/knowledge.md)  
**Philosopher:** [René Descartes](../thinkers/descartes-cogito.md)

## The Discovery
"I think, therefore I am" (*cogito ergo sum*) - the one truth that survives systematic [methodological doubt](../concepts/meta-philosophy/methodological-doubt.md) and provides an indubitable foundation for knowledge.

## Argument Structure

### The Method of Doubt
1. **Systematic doubt**: Question all beliefs that could possibly be false
2. **[Dreaming argument](descartes-dreaming-argument.md)**: Sensory experience unreliable
3. **[Evil demon hypothesis](descartes-evil-demon.md) ⚠️ Placeholder**: Even mathematical truths could be deceptions
4. **Universal doubt**: Attempt to doubt everything possible

### The Self-Refuting Nature of Doubt
1. **Act of doubting**: To doubt, one must think
2. **Thinking requires thinker**: Cannot have thoughts without a thinking substance
3. **Self-refutation**: "I doubt that I exist" is self-contradictory
4. **Immediate [certainty](../concepts/epistemology/certainty.md)**: The existence of the thinking self is indubitable

### The Cogito Discovery
- **Latin formulation**: *Cogito ergo sum* ("I think, therefore I am")
- **French formulation**: *Je pense, donc je suis* (Discourse on Method)
- **Not an inference**: Not a syllogism but an immediate intuition
- **Self-evident truth**: Known with complete [certainty](../concepts/epistemology/certainty.md) in the moment of thinking

## Philosophical Analysis

### What the Cogito Establishes
- **Existence of thinking self**: At minimum, a thinking substance exists
- **Immediate self-knowledge**: Direct awareness of own mental states
- **Temporal existence**: "I exist" is true whenever I think it
- **Foundation for knowledge**: Starting point for reconstructing certainty

### What the Cogito Doesn't Establish
- **Nature of the self**: What kind of thing the thinking self is
- **Bodily existence**: Whether the self has a physical body
- **Persistence through time**: Whether the same self continues existing
- **External world**: Whether anything exists beyond the thinking self

### Methodological vs Metaphysical Reading
- **Methodological**: Tool for finding certain foundations
- **Metaphysical**: Substantial claim about mind-body dualism
- **Interpretive debate**: How much metaphysics Descartes intended
- **Minimal vs maximal**: Conservative vs ambitious interpretations

## The Cogito's Logical Structure

### Not a Syllogism
- **Major premise**: Everything that thinks exists
- **Minor premise**: I think
- **Conclusion**: Therefore, I exist
- **Descartes' denial**: Not reasoning from universal to particular

### Immediate Intuition
- **Self-evident**: Truth apparent in the act of thinking itself
- **No middle term**: Direct awareness, not mediated by concepts
- **Performative**: Truth demonstrated in performance of thinking
- **Reflexive certainty**: Thinking turns back on itself

### Temporal Qualification
- **"While I am thinking"**: Cogito establishes existence only during thought
- **Momentary certainty**: Each moment of thinking confirms existence
- **Problem of continuity**: How to establish persistent self identity
- **Memory and imagination**: Routes to extended temporal existence

## Historical Context and Influences

### Augustinian Precedent
- **Augustine**: "If I am deceived, I am" (*Si fallor, sum*)
- **Anti-skeptical use**: Against Academic skeptics
- **Differences**: Augustine's religious context vs Descartes' scientific project
- **Recognition**: Descartes acknowledged Augustine's priority

### Medieval Background
- **Scholastic certainty**: Search for indubitable foundations
- **Aquinas on self-knowledge**: Immediate awareness of thinking
- **Differences**: Descartes' systematic doubt more radical
- **Innovation**: Methodological use of skepticism

## Contemporary Interpretations

### Analytical Philosophy
- **Russell**: Cogito establishes acquaintance knowledge
- **Ayer**: Cogito as tautology, not substantial discovery
- **Strawson**: Persons as primitive concept, not constructed from thinking
- **Critiques**: Various attacks on dualistic implications

### Phenomenological Tradition
- **Husserl**: Transcendental ego as foundation for phenomenology
- **Sartre**: Pre-reflective consciousness prior to cogito
- **Merleau-Ponty**: Embodied consciousness challenges Cartesian dualism
- **Development**: From Cartesian certainty to lived experience

### Postmodern Critiques
- **Nietzsche**: "I think" presupposes substantial self
- **Derrida**: Logocentrism and presence metaphysics
- **Foucault**: Subject as historical construction
- **Challenge**: Whether unified, substantial self exists

## Objections and Responses

### The Substantial Self Problem
- **Objection**: Cogito assumes substantial self rather than proving it
- **Lichtenberg**: Should say "It thinks" rather than "I think"
- **Response**: Some self-reference unavoidable in any thought
- **Minimal self**: Cogito establishes thinking subject, not substantial soul

### The Language Problem
- **Objection**: Cogito depends on meanings of words "I," "think," "exist"
- **Private language**: Can skeptical doubter rely on public language?
- **Response**: Immediate awareness doesn't require public language
- **Conceptual necessity**: Some concepts required for coherent thought

### The Circularity Problem
- **Objection**: Cogito uses reason to establish trustworthiness of reason
- **Divine guarantee**: God's existence needed to validate clear and distinct ideas
- **Cartesian circle**: Using God to validate reason, reason to prove God
- **Response**: Cogito doesn't depend on general reliability of reason

### The Temporal Problem
- **Objection**: Cogito establishes existence only momentarily
- **Hume's bundle theory**: No persistent self, only stream of thoughts
- **Kant's unity**: Transcendental unity of apperception
- **Contemporary**: Personal identity debates

## Applications and Extensions

### Foundationalism in Epistemology
- **Epistemic foundationalism**: Knowledge rests on indubitable foundations
- **Cogito as foundation**: Paradigm case of basic belief
- **Modern foundationalism**: Fallible foundations, contextual certainty
- **Critiques**: Coherentism, reliabilism, virtue epistemology

### Philosophy of Mind
- **Cartesian dualism**: Mind as thinking substance distinct from matter
- **Knowledge of other minds**: Argument by analogy from self-knowledge
- **Consciousness studies**: First-person authority and phenomenal consciousness
- **Self-knowledge**: Special access to own mental states

### Artificial Intelligence
- **Machine consciousness**: Can computers achieve cogito-like self-awareness?
- **Turing test**: Behavioral vs first-person criteria for thinking
- **Chinese room**: Syntax vs semantics in artificial cognition
- **Hard problem**: Subjective experience vs computational processing

## Contemporary Relevance

### Consciousness Studies
- **Hard problem**: Explaining subjective experience
- **Zombie arguments**: Conceivability of consciousness without behavior
- **First-person methodology**: Role of introspection in studying consciousness
- **Neural correlates**: Brain basis of self-awareness

### Personal Identity
- **Psychological continuity**: Memory and personality as basis for identity
- **Narrative identity**: Self as constructed through storytelling
- **Split-brain cases**: Multiple streams of consciousness in single brain
- **Philosophy of psychiatry**: Disorders of self-experience

### Technology and Virtual Reality
- **Digital consciousness**: Could uploaded minds achieve cogito?
- **Virtual reality**: Distinction between real and simulated experience
- **Social media**: Construction of digital selves
- **AI ethics**: Rights for potentially conscious artificial agents

## Significance for A Fortiori
The cogito exemplifies reasoning from the most basic possible [foundation](../concepts/meta-philosophy/foundation.md) to establish stronger conclusions. It demonstrates how systematic doubt can lead to discovery of indubitable starting points.

## Key Questions

- What kind of self does the cogito establish?
- Can the cogito serve as foundation for knowledge of external world?
- Is immediate self-knowledge privileged over other forms of knowledge?
